The Company

Family-owned private transportation into the parts of Western North Carolina other services refuse.

WNC Shuttle is a family-owned private transportation company based in Waynesville, North Carolina, operated by Justin.

OBSERVED

The brand promise is the tagline: “We go where others won’t… or can’t.” The differentiator is reaching remote, unpaved, steep, or seasonal roads in the Blue Ridge that ride-share and ordinary car services cannot.

OBSERVED

Primary contact channel is the phone line, (828) 400-3987 — calls and texts answered personally. The personal voice is core to the brand (“family-owned, talk to a real person”).

OBSERVED

Positioning is deliberately anti-Uber: fixed published pricing factors, no rate negotiation, no surge, personal service, discretion.

OBSERVED

Service rules from the FAQ: no pets, no limo-style service, and no wheelchair-accessible vehicle — these inquiries must currently be declined by hand.

OBSERVED

The business is capacity-bound by one human’s calendar — it closed entirely over a holiday for a family event. Justin’s attention is the scarce resource.

INFERRED

The real moat is not the vehicles — it is undocumented road knowledge: which roads each vehicle can survive, by season and weather. Today that knowledge lives in one head.

INFERRED

Revenue mix by trip type (airport vs. trailhead vs. casino vs. wedding) is not precisely known — even, we suspect, to Justin.

ASSUMED

Service Area & Demand Sources

Where the trips come from, and when.

Demand sources

  • Asheville Regional Airport (AVL) runs — including recurring 5am departures; likely the volume backbone. INFERRED
  • Trailhead drops & pickups — hikers into the remote Blue Ridge (Cataloochee and similar); the signature “we go where others won’t” trips. OBSERVED
  • Harrah’s Cherokee casino trips. OBSERVED
  • Weddings — venues around Maggie Valley; group shuttle logistics. INFERRED
  • Cabin-rental guests (VRBO/Airbnb) staying at remote properties. INFERRED
  • Anglers — fly-fishing access to remote water (Pigeon River corridor). ASSUMED

Seasonality & events

  • Leaf season (fall) is peak demand. INFERRED
  • Cataloochee ski season (winter) brings its own traffic and road risk. INFERRED
  • Apple Festival and the WNC festival calendar drive spikes — a past 10%-off-hourly festival promotion worked but was never systematized. OBSERVED
  • Demand is lumpy against fixed capacity — so the economic enemy is deadhead miles and idle hours, not customer count. INFERRED

Terrain constraints (hard requirements for any software)

  • Cell coverage is absent across much of the service area — anything driver-facing must work fully offline and sync later. OBSERVED
  • Google ETAs are badly wrong on mountain roads (a last mile can take 22 minutes, not Google’s 8) — a proprietary travel-time model is a potential moat. INFERRED
  • Road passability varies by vehicle, season, and weather — ice, fog, downed trees; a wrong acceptance isn’t a bad review, it can be a rescue. INFERRED
  • The Blue Ridge Parkway closes at dusk seasonally and routes must adapt (e.g. take NC-276 instead). ASSUMED

Partner ecosystem (mapped in Phase 2)

  • Cabin hosts — touch the guest months before Justin can. INFERRED
  • Harrah’s Cherokee concierge — potential standing “safe ride home” arrangement. ASSUMED
  • Fly-fishing guides & outfitters — symbionts, not competitors. ASSUMED
  • Wedding coordinators in Maggie Valley. ASSUMED
  • Neighboring operators (Bryson City, Franklin) for out-of-range trips. ASSUMED

05

How the Operation Actually Runs

The workflow from inquiry to payment, as we currently understand it.

Inquiries arrive by phone, text, email, voicemail, and the booking tool. After-hours inquiries wait until morning — and some callers book a competitor within minutes (the 11pm voicemail answered at 8am).

INFERRED

Justin personally gates every confirmation — a roughly 30-second check per booking. What he verifies in those 30 seconds (vehicle fit, road passability, schedule fit, client trust) is the undocumented core business logic of the company. The gate is a safety system, not friction.

INFERRED

The same trip is rekeyed into multiple systems: booking tool, calendar, confirmation email, invoice. This is the pain that sent him toward Zapier.

OBSERVED

Trips are not instrumented: no log of actual vs. predicted travel time, no record of refused rides, no record of why quotes were lost.

ASSUMED

Confirmation emails are written by hand, often with route-specific local color — a quality worth preserving in any automation.

ASSUMED
The two metrics that move money

Round-trip attach rate (kill the deadhead mile at quote time) and admin-hours-removed (every admin hour automated becomes sellable capacity). Both come straight out of the Phase 2 synthesis and shape how Phase 3 will score ideas.

06

Working Principles Carried Into Phase 3

Session-derived rules that constrain every idea we evaluate.

Design principles

  • Data before intelligence. The trip log, lost-quote log, and refused-ride log are free to start and every AI idea depends on them.
  • Single-entry. One canonical trip object every downstream system reads and writes. Design the schema before buying the glue.
  • The autonomy ladder. Every feature ships as draft-for-human, graduates to human-approves after 50 correct drafts, and only ever reaches autonomous for reversible or strictly grounded actions.
  • Buy back attention, or productize knowledge. Every idea must do one of the two — that is the filter.

Red lines

  • The confirmation gate stays human. AI may pre-clear the easy 80% and escalate the rest — it may not book unsupervised.
  • No AI on the main phone line. The personal voice is the product; the projected cost of automating it is repeat-booking erosion.
  • No surge or dynamic pricing. It collides head-on with “we do not negotiate rates.”
  • Reliability beats cleverness. Every automation is a system Justin must debug at 4am before an airport run.
  • Offline-first for anything used in the field.

Open Questions to Verify with Justin

The assumptions above, converted into the interview list. Answers here will re-rank the ideas in Phase 3.
# verify-before-building.txt
  • Q1Booking volume & mix. Trips per week by type (airport / trailhead / casino / wedding / other), and how it swings by season?
  • Q2The 30-second check. Walk through the last ten confirmations — what exactly was checked, and what would have made you decline?
  • Q3bookingtool.net exit ramp. Does it offer CSV export, webhooks, or an API? Who owns the customer records contractually?
  • Q4Quote funnel. How many quotes go out per week, how many convert, and does anything record the ones that don’t?
  • Q5Drivers. How many active 1099 drivers today, which vehicles/roads is each trusted on, and how long did the last onboarding take?
  • Q6Tool reality check. Is Google Calendar actually the schedule of record? Is Stripe used on every trip or only some? Is Zapier already connected to anything?
  • Q7Refused & missed demand. Roughly how often do you decline a trip (wrong vehicle, too far, booked solid), and where do those callers go?
  • Q8The road knowledge. Would you sit for a recorded 30-minute interview about mountain roads — and are you comfortable with that becoming structured data?
  • Q9Round trips. What share of airport runs today are booked as round trips, and is a return-leg discount ever offered at quote time?
  • Q10Partners. Which cabin hosts, venues, or guides already refer you informally — and who would you never want to be contractually tied to?
