How the grouping works

Each idea has exactly one primary group — the reason you’d build it — and overlap shows up as “also:” tags instead of a filing argument. The four outcome groups are Fill the Vehicle (utilization — the save-money bucket, reframed), Buy Back Justin’s Time (admin hours become sellable capacity), Grow the Market (new demand), and Build the Asset (things that make the business itself more valuable, like the concierge). A fifth group, Foundation, protects the enablers — data capture and schema work that produce no direct outcome but that everything else depends on.

Every card carries an effort chip ($0 / small / medium / large), the metric it moves, and any dependency. Ideas that violate a red line from the context dossier are parked at the bottom, not deleted.

Foundation

No direct payoff — and everything above depends on them. Protected from outcome-scoring on purpose.

The Phase 2 verdict was blunt: no corpus, no intelligence. These nine start first, and most are free.

One trip record every system reads and writes — the single-entry schema that makes every later automation trivial instead of a translation layer. The architecture decision of the whole project.

V

Fill the Vehicle

Utilization — deadhead miles and idle hours are the enemy, not customer count. The “save money” group, reframed.

With three vehicles and fixed capacity, round-trip attach rate is the highest-leverage number in the business.

V1Return-leg attach at quote time

Half of every airport run is paid fuel and driver at zero revenue. Offer the return leg to the same client, in the same quote, at a discount. Not dynamic pricing — a published round-trip rate.

SMALLalso: money
moves → round-trip attach rate (the #1 metric)

V2Standing corridor contracts

The deadhead is predictable — same corridor, same time. Match it against scheduled demand (pharmacy runs, document courier for Waynesville businesses) as standing contracts, not a marketplace.

MEDIUMalso: market
moves → revenue per deadhead mile

V3The flex-window rate

Client accepts a 90-minute pickup window for ~25% off; the flexibility lets the schedule pack tighter. AI proposes the windows; Justin approves. A published rate class, not a negotiation.

MEDIUMalso: money
moves → trips per vehicle-day

V4The cancellation waitlist

A cancellation at 25 hours out currently leaves a hole nothing fills. Instantly offer the freed slot to the lost-quote log (F5) — turning yesterday’s no into today’s yes.

MEDIUMneeds F5
moves → recovered revenue per cancellation

V5Daily assignment optimizer

Given tomorrow’s confirmed rides, propose the vehicle-to-trip assignment that minimizes deadhead miles. With 3 vehicles the problem is small enough to solve exactly; Justin gets one approval.

MEDIUMalso: timeneeds F1
moves → deadhead miles per day

V6Capacity broadcast

A simple “I have capacity Saturday” signal to the partner network (cabin hosts, venues, guides) — the mycelial chemical signal. Beats any ad because it reaches people mid-booking.

SMALLalso: market
moves → fill rate on soft days
T

Buy Back Justin’s Time

Attention is the scarce resource. Every admin hour removed is sellable capacity — saving time IS revenue here.

The gate stays human. The point is that the easy 80% stops touching it, and nothing leaks at 11pm.

T1After-hours capture

The 11pm voicemail answered at 8am already booked a competitor at 11:15. An after-hours responder texts back an estimate and a promise of human confirmation, gathers trip details, never quotes a binding price.

BUILD AMEDIUMalso: market
moves → after-hours lead recovery rate

T2FAQ-grounded chatbot

Grounded strictly in the unusually thorough FAQ page — the cheapest, safest AI win available, and it converts. Answers the questions that precede every booking, 24/7.

BUILD ASMALLalso: market
moves → inquiries answered without Justin

T3Inquiry triage classifier

Score every inbound inquiry bookable / needs-Justin / decline. Auto-decline pet and wheelchair requests politely and instantly, with a referral to someone who can serve them. The boring classifier that 2030-Justin said mattered most.

MEDIUM
moves → interruptions per day

T4One-tap confirmation pre-clear

Using the encoded gate rules (F4), AI pre-checks the easy 80% and sends Justin an approve/deny SMS with a drafted summary and recommendation. The gate stays human; the friction doesn’t.

MEDIUMneeds F4
moves → seconds per confirmation

T5Phone-line wrap

A Twilio number that forwards to Justin unchanged but records, transcribes, and summarizes every call into a lead row. The line still rings a human — the data just stops evaporating.

SMALL
moves → leads captured per call

T6Drafted confirmation email

AI drafts the confirmation with route-specific local color (“we’ll take 276, not the Parkway — it closes at dusk this time of year”); Justin approves and sends. Interim $0 version: turn on the booking tool’s own confirmation email.

SMALLalso: asset
moves → minutes per booking

T7Quote-assistant webform

Asks the four questions the booking tool doesn’t (luggage, pace, road surface, kids) and pre-fills a real quote for Justin to confirm — fewer back-and-forth texts per quote.

MEDIUM
moves → touches per quote

T8Lost-quote follow-up

An AI-drafted follow-up per lost quote with a specific objection guess (price, timing, road uncertainty) — plus a quote-to-book model that tells Justin which three quotes to personally call today.

SMALLalso: marketneeds F5
moves → quote conversion

T9Ride-complete automation

Trip completion triggers Stripe capture, receipt, and a Google-review text 90 minutes after drop-off — when warmth is highest. Wait-time surcharges computed from GPS dwell and evidenced.

SMALLalso: market · money
moves → review count + days-to-payment

T10Flight-delay agent

Watches the flight feed; on a delay it drafts the client SMS, reshuffles the day’s schedule, and presents Justin one approval. Extends the flight tracking that already exists internally.

LARGEneeds F1
moves → 5am scrambles per month

T11Driver ops kit

Merged: shared-Sheet dispatch board today, offline-first PWA later (schedule, nav link, checklist, drop photo); AI-generated 1099 handbook, route briefings, and a road-certification quiz — onboarding in 45 minutes, not two weeks. Offline policy assistant in the field.

MEDIUMalso: asset
moves → driver onboarding time · cross-coverage
M

Grow the Market

New demand — through partners who touch the guest first, and owned channels instead of rented ones.

The mycelial rule: grow the network of referrers, not the fleet. Ten cabin hosts beat one more Navigator.

M1Cabin-host channel

Hosts touch the guest three months before Justin could. $0 start: call every host he’s driven a guest for and ask for a link. Scale-up: a co-branded transport page and referral code per host.

$0 STARTSMALL TO SCALE
moves → bookings at cabin-booking time, not landing time

M2The Harrah’s ask

Ask the Harrah’s Cherokee concierge for a standing “safe ride home” arrangement — recurring revenue at zero marketing cost. The worst answer is no.

moves → recurring corridor revenue

M3Outfitter & guide hand-offs

Fly-fishing guides and outfitters are symbionts, not competitors — they need clients delivered to remote water. A shared booking hand-off; later, river-condition nudges to past anglers with one-tap rebook.

MEDIUM
moves → referred trips per season

M4Wedding coordinator tool

Give Maggie Valley venue coordinators a free AI-built “guest transport plan” generator — and become the default shuttle on every plan it produces.

MEDIUMalso: asset

M5Partner operator network

For trips too far from base: a Bryson City or Franklin operator, revenue-shared, disclosed to the client, contractually held to standard. The opposite of a farm-out — and it monetizes the refused-ride log (F6).

MEDIUMneeds F6

M6Owned-content rebuild

Re-publish the 10 dead posts with updated dates and internal links ($0); AI-drafted seasonal pages published six weeks before demand spikes (leaf, ski, Apple Festival), syndicated to Facebook — own the asset, rent the reach. Hand-approved pages only; no programmatic flood.

$0 STARTSMALL

M7GBP & review engine

Three sample routes with real prices. The FAQ already explains the pricing factors — publishing examples removes the largest silent objection at zero cost.

$0

Slime-mold routing: track which referral sources actually convert and starve the rest. Plus the one-question text to the last 100 clients — “what almost stopped you from booking?” — which is the marketing roadmap.

A

Build the Asset

Things that make the business itself more valuable — the moat, the memory, and the experiences nobody else can copy.

The moat is not the 4x4s — it is knowing which roads the 4x4s can survive. Digitizing that is risk mitigation, training set, and the only licensable asset, all at once.

A1The Mountain Road Brain

A simple table first — road, vehicle, season, verdict, last-verified, driver note — filled by a weekly hour-long interview agent that mines Justin’s memory chunk by chunk. The most boring idea in the field, and probably the most valuable. Licensable by 2030.

MEDIUMalso: time · marketseeded by F7
moves → the succession risk & the moat

A2Mountain travel-time model

Trained on the trip logs (F2) to correct Google’s badly wrong ETAs — the 22-minute last mile Google calls 8. Prevents the 5am airport miss; becomes proprietary data nobody else can buy.

LARGEneeds a season of F2 data
moves → on-time rate · quote accuracy

A3Client memory

A lightweight CRM the AI maintains: who repeats, who hates small talk, who wants the history of Cataloochee. A one-line preference note at trip start is the cheapest luxury upgrade in the business.

SMALLalso: time
moves → repeat-booking rate

A4Trailhead Concierge

Name the hike; it plans drop point, pickup point, pace buffer, and weather bail-out. Turns a ride into a planned experience — the premium product for the “we go where others won’t” trips that carry 3× margin.

MEDIUMalso: marketneeds A1
moves → premium-trip share of revenue

A5Road-condition risk score

The night before each trip: ice, fog, downed-tree risk scored per route, flagging what to re-plan. Safety encoded — vital when the differentiator is safety and the drivers are contractors.

MEDIUMneeds A1
moves → incidents avoided · re-plans before 4am

A6In-vehicle offline guide

“What’s that mountain?” answered with no cell signal — a luxury touch that works precisely where the service area is. Pairs with the dashcam driver scorecard for the safety story.

MEDIUM
moves → the story clients retell

A7Revenue-mix analytics

Auto-categorize every past trip (airport / trailhead / casino / wedding) to reveal the real revenue mix — which nobody precisely knows. Monthly “what people asked for that we couldn’t serve” report from the logs.

SMALLalso: foundationneeds F8
moves → where the software points next

The Narrowing

Scored by effort against the two money metrics — round-trip attach rate and admin-hours-removed — and checked against the red lines.
Tier 1 · Do now

Six moves — nearly all free, all compounding

  • F3+F4Map the workflow and encode the gate. One sit-down produces both. Everything else is scoped by what this reveals.
  • F5+F6+F7Start the three logs and record the seed corpus. Lost quotes, refused rides, road talk — the free data collection that must start today.
  • F1+F2Build the trip object with silent time-logging. Phase 2’s BUILD B, unchanged. Small, boring, foundational.
  • T2+T1Ship the FAQ chatbot, then after-hours capture. Phase 2’s BUILD A — the converting win, in two steps with the chatbot first.
  • V1Put the round-trip rate in every airport quote. The single highest-leverage pricing change, and it’s a rate card edit.
  • M1+M2+M7+M8Run the $0 market plays. Cabin-host link asks, the Harrah’s ask, GBP + in-car review asks, fare examples on the site.
Tier 2 · Next — after Tier 1 data exists

The attention buy-backs and the first asset work

  • T4One-tap confirmation pre-clear — as soon as F4’s rules hold up against real bookings.
  • T5+T6+T9Phone wrap, drafted confirmations, ride-complete automation — the admin loop, closed.
  • T8+V4Work the lost-quote log — follow-ups and the cancellation waitlist.
  • A1Start the Mountain Road Brain — the weekly interview hour begins once the seed corpus proves the format.
  • A3Client memory — preference notes riding on the trip object.
  • F8+A7Ingestion layer & revenue-mix analytics — gated on the bookingtool.net export answer (Q3).
Tier 3 · Later — needs data, partners, or a season of proof

The compounders

  • A2Travel-time model — after a season of F2 logs.
  • A4+A5Trailhead Concierge & risk scoring — after the Road Brain has real coverage.
  • V2+V3+V5Corridor contracts, flex windows, assignment optimizer — utilization mechanics once volume data exists.
  • M3+M4+M5Guide, wedding & operator partnerships — capped at 8–12 active partners; each is a human relationship.
  • T3+T10+T11Triage classifier, flight agent, driver ops kit — worth it at higher volume or the next driver hire.
  • A6In-vehicle guide — the luxury flourish, after the fundamentals.
The shape of the cut

Tier 1 costs almost nothing and is mostly data capture plus the two Phase 2 build picks. Every Tier 2 and 3 item consumes what Tier 1 produces. If Tier 1 stalls, nothing above it is worth building — which is exactly the order of operations the $0 Mandate predicted.

# parked.txt — reopen only if the stated condition changes
  • ✕Dynamic / surge pricing. Collides with “we do not negotiate rates.” Published rate classes (round-trip, flex-window) deliver the economics without the brand damage. Reopen: never — the discounts stay published.
  • ✕Voice AI answering the main line. The voice IS the product; projected cost is repeat-booking erosion. T1/T5 capture the value without answering as Justin. Reopen: only for a separate overflow line, clearly not-Justin.
  • ✕Backhaul marketplace. A 3-vehicle fleet has no liquidity. V2’s standing contracts are the tractable version. Reopen: at 10+ vehicles or a real operator coalition.
  • ✕Shared per-seat AVL shuttle. Breaks the word “private” in the value proposition. Reopen: as a separate brand, AVL corridor only.
  • ✕Dashcam scenic reels for clients. Consent and privacy problem in a business built on discretion. Reopen: opt-in only, if clients ask for it.
  • ✕Predictive staffing. 1099 status legally cannot compel held availability. Reopen: if the driver model changes.
  • ✕Programmatic SEO at scale. Thin-content penalty risk on a 10-post domain. M6’s hand-approved seasonal pages are the safe version. Reopen: page-by-page, never as a flood.
  • ✕Fleet digital twin / OBD maintenance prediction. Real, but three vehicles don’t amortize it; a maintenance calendar does 90% of the job. Reopen: at vehicle #5.
